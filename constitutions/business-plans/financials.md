# Financials and the ask

The revenue the plan expects, the unit economics behind it, the cash it burns, and how much
the company raises and what that money reaches.

## Revenue

Revenue is built bottom-up: customers times price, with churn taken off and expansion added
on. The customers come from go-to-market capacity in `business-model.md` — leads times
conversion rates, or sales hires times quota times ramp — so each year's new customers are
what the plan's own spend can reach. A curve that steepens with no driver steepening under
it is the shape investors discount first.

The Y5 customer count is the SOM in [market](market.md). A revenue model that needs more
customers than the SOM holds disagrees with the market section, and one of them is wrong.

The terms are defined once and held: recognised revenue, bookings, and ARR are different
figures, and ARR counts only recurring revenue — a one-off setup fee annualised is not ARR.

## Unit economics

The metrics are defined in the file, with the formula, because investors compute them
differently and a figure without its definition cannot be compared:

- **Gross margin** — revenue less the cost of revenue from [costs](costs.md), over revenue.
- **CAC** — sales and marketing spend, people included, over new customers in the period.
- **LTV** — average revenue per customer × gross margin ÷ churn rate.
- **LTV:CAC**, **CAC payback** in months, **net revenue retention**, and **burn multiple**
  (net burn over net new ARR).

Each is compared with a benchmark for the company's own category, cited with its source and
date. The category matters: an AI-native product carries inference in its cost of revenue
and runs a lower gross margin than classic SaaS, and measured against the classic figure it
looks broken when it is merely typical. Benchmarks move year to year, which is why the date
is part of the citation.

## Statements

Five years of income statement, annual; cash and burn monthly for the first 24 months;
headcount alongside. The file states the month the company breaks even, or the month it
needs the next round, and whichever comes first.

## The ask

The ask states:

- the amount, and the instrument where it is decided;
- the use of funds by category — people, product, go-to-market, other — summing to the
  amount and matching [costs](costs.md) over the same months;
- the runway it buys at the planned burn, which covers reaching the milestones plus the
  months the next raise takes to close — 18 to 24 months at seed in 2026 guidance;
- the milestones it reaches: measurable ones a next-round investor would fund on, such as
  revenue, customer count, retention, or a product shipped to a named segment.

> $2.0M seed: 62% people (6 hires, see team), 18% product and infrastructure, 15%
> go-to-market, 5% legal and other. 22 months of runway at planned burn. Reaches $1.2M ARR,
> 120 paying firms and 110% net revenue retention — the Series A bar for the category as of
> the plan date.

not

> We are raising $2M to accelerate growth and scale the team.

## Scenarios

The downside from [costs](costs.md) runs through revenue and cash as well. The file shows
the runway under it, and the trigger — a month, a revenue figure — at which the plan changes
course, such as pausing hires, and what that change saves.
