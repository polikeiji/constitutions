# Team

Who builds the company, what each person owns, which roles are not yet filled, and when
each is hired.

## Members

Each member carries: name, role, what they own — the decisions and outcomes, not a list of
tasks — their commitment, and the experience that makes them the right person for this
company in particular.

Commitment is a date and a fraction: *full-time from 2027-01*, *two days a week until the
seed closes*. A founder who is not yet full-time is stated as such. Investors ask, and a
plan that implied otherwise loses more than the answer would have.

Experience is written as outcomes relevant to this plan — *built and ran the billing
platform for 4M users*, *sold to hospital procurement for eight years* — rather than as
titles and employer logos. What makes a team fundable at this stage is the fit between the
people and this problem, and an employer's name does not show it.

A person is named only with their agreement.

## Advisors and investors

Advisors and existing investors are listed apart from the team, each with what they
actually contribute: introductions to a named channel, a regulatory review, hours a month.
They are not counted in the team or the headcount; a board of well-known names without a
stated contribution reads as decoration.

## Roles not yet filled

Every role the plan needs and nobody holds is listed as **TBD** — never a placeholder name,
and never a candidate who has not signed. Each carries:

- what it owns, and the gap in the current team it closes;
- when it is hired: a quarter, tied to the milestone or funding event that triggers it;
- the profile it hires for, and where the person sits — location sets the cost;
- whether it is an employee or a contractor.

A candidate in talks may be described as *in discussion* if they agree, and joins the
member list when they commit.

A plan whose founding team has no one owning a function the business depends on — selling,
building, a regulated activity — names that gap here rather than leaving an investor to find
it. The TBD role that closes it is the answer.

## The headcount plan

This file owns the headcount plan: every role, founders included, and the quarter each
starts, over five years. Roles hired in the first 24 months — the period the raise has to
fund — are listed one by one; Y3 to Y5 may group them by function (*engineering +3 in Y3*).

[Costs](costs.md) applies salaries to this plan and does not keep a second one. A hire moved
here moves its cost there.

## What stays out

Individual salaries and each person's equity stay out of the plan. Salary appears in the
cost model as a band per role; the cap table goes to an investor in diligence, not in the
plan.
